\begin{abstract}
The Global Gas Model (GGM) is a partial equilibrium model of the world natural gas market.
Most of the expert work in using it lies not in the model but around it: preparing more than 15,000 input values for each study, setting up scenarios, calibrating the model by hand over several days, and interpreting the results.
This thesis asks where in that workflow LLM-based agents could take over or support the work, where ordinary code would serve better, and where the work should stay with a person.

The workflow is analysed task by task, drawing on the model's documentation, code and 2023 input data, a working session with one of its developers, and the literature on agents that operate and calibrate simulation models.
The division between agents, code and people turns out to run within the steps of the workflow rather than between them.
Agents suit the parts that involve reading and judging unstructured material, such as extracting values from reports, mapping sources to the model's nodes, diagnosing calibration deviations and drafting explanations of results; most of the effort lies in the data, which is also where they fit best.
Ordinary code should do every computation and every model run.
People should remain responsible for the question and storyline of a scenario, the acceptance of the calibration, and the interpretation of the results.
Calibration marks the limit, and is where the thesis extends the existing literature: unlike the measurable parameters in earlier studies, GGM's calibrated parameters carry economic meaning and are not pinned down by the data, so an agent can diagnose and propose, but accepting a calibration remains an economic judgment.

On this basis the thesis proposes a system of four agents that work alongside the analyst, attach reasons, sources and confidence to every suggestion, and keep the analyst's corrections as lessons for later work.
These principles do not depend on GGM and could carry over to other policy models.
It ends with the risks of this division of labour and a plan for building and testing the system in the master's thesis.
\end{abstract}
