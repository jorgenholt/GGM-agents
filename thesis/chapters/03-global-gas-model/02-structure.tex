\section{How the model is built}

The central players are suppliers, which produce gas at one or more nodes, sell it at home or abroad, and buy transport and storage services to get it there \citep[p.~48]{egging2019global}.
Each supplier's production capacity is split into three tranches of increasing cost, and capacity is set exogenously.
Gas moves between nodes along arcs: pipelines, or a chain of liquefaction, shipping and regasification for LNG, with storage at the nodes.
Pipeline, LNG and storage capacities can be expanded within limits, and new capacity becomes available one period after the investment decision.
Each consumption node has a linear inverse demand curve for each season, derived from a reference consumption, a reference price and fixed price elasticities for four demand sectors \citep[pp.~32--33]{egging2019global}.

Market power enters through a conjectural variation parameter between 0 and 1 for each supplier, node and year, where 0 means perfect competition and 1 Cournot behaviour.
As Section 1.4 noted, the resulting equilibrium is computed as the solution of one quadratic optimisation problem, which maximises sales revenue and consumer surplus minus production and infrastructure costs, corrected by a market power adjustment term \citep[pp.~47--49]{egging2019global}.
This is a reformulation of the original complementarity problem that yields the same equilibrium but is faster to solve for a model of this size.

The model is large.
The 2023 dataset provided by the developers describes 125 countries through 239 nodes, including separate nodes for 37 liquefaction and 66 regasification terminals, with 267 pipelines, more than 2,400 possible shipping routes generated by the code, and 11 suppliers with market power.
The time horizon runs from 2020 to 2060 in five-year steps, with three seasons per year.
The current public version is written in Julia and solved with the commercial solver Gurobi \citep{ggmpublicongithubndggm}.
One run of a single scenario, which we performed on a laptop, built a problem with about 5.5 million variables and 1.9 million constraints and took 24 minutes, of which 18 were spent in the solver.
