\section{Approach}

This chapter reviews what has been done with agents built on large language models (LLMs) around large, existing domain models.
Its purpose is to place the thesis in that work and to end by stating the gap the rest of the thesis addresses.

\subsection{What the chapter covers}

The relationship that matters here is the one between an agent and a model that already exists.
In every case reviewed below, the domain model is built by people, is trusted by its users, and stays as it is.
The agent works around it: it writes the model's inputs, runs it, adjusts its parameters, explains its results, or decides when to stop and ask a person.
This is the same relationship this thesis proposes between agents and the Global Gas Model (GGM), which is not to be changed.

The chapter is therefore organised by \emph{what the agent was asked to do}, not by application domain.
Organising by domain would turn the chapter into a survey of AI in energy, nuclear engineering or hydrology, and would hide the fact that the same few tasks recur across all of them.
The sections follow roughly the order in which the work around a model happens: setting up and running a simulation code (Section 2.2), calibrating a model against targets (Section 2.3), querying and explaining an optimisation model (Section 2.4), and building scenarios across several energy and economic models (Section 2.5).
Section 2.6 turns from what agents do to how the work is divided between agents, ordinary code and people, drawing on recent work on AI-assisted research in economics and the social sciences.
Section 2.7 states the gap.

The chapter is written for a reader who has only the short sketch of GGM in Section 1.4.
Where a paper is relevant to a particular step of the GGM workflow, that link is made in Chapter 5, which cites back to this chapter rather than describing the paper again.

\subsection{How the literature was found}

The literature was collected in September and October 2026 by searching arXiv, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and Crossref, and by web search for practitioner and institutional sources.
Searches combined terms for LLM agents with terms for calibration, history matching, input generation, simulation codes, optimisation models, energy-system models, equilibrium models and economic research.
The reference lists of the closest papers were then followed backwards and forwards.
Papers judged closest to the thesis were read in full; the rest were assessed from their abstracts and, where needed, their method sections.

Two features of this literature shape how it should be read.
First, it is very recent.
Most of the papers closest to this thesis appeared in 2025 and 2026, and many are preprints that have not yet been peer reviewed.
Where a peer-reviewed version exists, it is cited instead; otherwise the preprint is cited and its status noted.
Second, the models the papers evaluate are often superseded within months.
Reported numbers are therefore read as evidence about \emph{patterns} of what works and what fails, not as stable measures of what current models can do.

\subsection{Terminology}

Three terms need fixing before the review.
An \emph{LLM agent}, or simply an agent, is used here in the sense common in the recent literature: a language model that calls tools in a loop to reach a goal, observing the result of each call before choosing the next \citep{yao2023react, willison2025i}.
Chapter 4 develops the definition and distinguishes agents from fixed workflows in which code, not the model, decides the sequence of steps \citep{schluntz2024building}.
A \emph{domain model} is the simulation or optimisation model the agent works around, such as a reactor code, a hydrological model or GGM.
\emph{Calibration} means adjusting a model's uncertain inputs until its outputs match reference values; in petroleum engineering the same task is called history matching.\footnote{The word \emph{agent} also has two other meanings nearby: an agent in an agent-based simulation \citep{gao2024llms}, and a player in an equilibrium model, such as GGM's producers and traders.
In this thesis, \emph{agent} on its own always means an LLM agent; GGM's agents are called players.}
