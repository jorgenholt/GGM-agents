\section{Approach}

This chapter reviews work in which agents built on large language models (LLMs) operate around an existing domain model: a simulation or optimisation model that the agent configures, runs, calibrates or explains, but does not change.
That is the relationship this thesis proposes between agents and the Global Gas Model (GGM).
The chapter is organised by what the agent was asked to do rather than by application domain, because the same few tasks recur across very different fields.

Sources were found in September and October 2026 by searching arXiv, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and Crossref, and by following the reference lists of the closest studies.
A study was included if an LLM configures, runs, calibrates or explains a model that it neither creates nor changes.
Studies in which an LLM writes a new model from a problem description, imitates people inside a simulation, or replaces a model as a forecaster were excluded.
Work on calibration and on human oversight of automation from before LLMs was added where it frames the recent studies.

The field is very young: most of the studies closest to this thesis are 2026 preprints, evaluated by their own authors on one or a few cases.
They are therefore read for patterns in what works and what fails, rather than for their performance figures.

Throughout, an \emph{agent} means a language model that pursues a goal by repeatedly choosing an action, such as calling a tool or running a model, and observing the result before choosing the next \citep{yao2023react}.\footnote{In equilibrium modelling, \emph{agent} can also mean a market player.
To avoid confusion, GGM's market players are called suppliers in this thesis.}
