\subsection{How the field evaluates itself}

Read together, the studies share several features of method that limit what can be concluded from them.
Most are evaluated by their authors, on benchmarks or cases they chose, and few compare the agent with established methods or with human practitioners; HydroAgent, Data2BEM and the grey-box calibration study are the main exceptions.
Many rest on one or a few cases, and where synthetic and real cases are both reported, as in PetroGraph, the real case is much harder.
Success is usually defined as a run that completes or a fit that is close, which the more careful authors themselves describe as necessary but not sufficient \citep{li2026moosenger, li2026optargus}.
And the human role is often designed but not studied: PetroGraph's checkpoints were skipped, the auditability of the grey-box study's rationales was argued rather than tested, and AutoSAM left the comparison with human analysts to future work.
Only the experiment by \citet{zhu2026human} varies the division of work between people, code and the model and measures the effect.

Two consequences follow for this thesis.
First, the evidence supports claims about \emph{patterns}, such as the value of a harness and of deterministic computation, more firmly than claims about how well a given agent performs.
Second, an agent working around GGM would need an evaluation designed for the case where a close fit is not enough.
Chapter 5 uses the literature in the first way, and the plan for the master's thesis in Chapter 8 takes up the second.
