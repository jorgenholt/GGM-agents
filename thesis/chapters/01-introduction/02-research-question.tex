The thesis addresses one main question:

\begin{quote}
Where in the workflow around the Global Gas Model could LLM-based agents take over or support expert work, where would ordinary code serve better, and where should the work stay with a person?
\end{quote}

It is broken into four sub-questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item What does the workflow around GGM consist of, and where in it do the expert effort and judgment lie? (Chapter 3)
  \item What kinds of tasks are LLM agents suited to, and which are better done by ordinary code? (Chapter 4)
  \item For each step of the GGM workflow, what could an agent do, and where must a person remain involved? (Chapter 5)
  \item What would it take for an agent to support the calibration of a model whose calibrated parameters carry economic judgment, and what must the person overseeing it decide? (Chapters 5 and 7)
\end{enumerate}

The first three sub-questions cover the workflow as a whole, which is the scope of the thesis.
The fourth singles out calibration, because it is where the gap identified in Chapter 2 lies and where the thesis expects its most general findings.
Chapter 6 brings the answers together as a sketch of the system the master's thesis would build.
