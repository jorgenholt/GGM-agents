\section{Contribution and structure}

The main contribution is a step-by-step map of the GGM workflow showing, for each step, what an agent could do, what ordinary code should do, and where a person should stay involved, ending with a description of each proposed agent as the starting point for the master's thesis.
Beyond GGM, the thesis applies to a market equilibrium model a division of labour that recent evidence supports elsewhere, and examines a case the literature on agent-assisted calibration has not addressed: parameters that carry economic meaning and are not pinned down by the data.
It argues that a person must then remain responsible for accepting the calibration, because the decision is partly an economic judgment.

Chapter 2 reviews the literature and states the gap.
Chapter 3 describes GGM and its workflow, and Chapter 4 what LLM agents can and cannot do.
Chapter 5, the core of the thesis, brings the two together step by step.
Chapter 6 discusses the risks, what should not be automated and the limitations of the analysis, and sets out the plan for the master's thesis.
