\section{Contribution}

The main contribution of this thesis is a map of where AI can be used across the workflow around GGM, and of the reasoning behind each placement.
The workflow is followed step by step, from obtaining source data through preparing it, setting up a scenario and calibrating the model, to running it and reading the results.
At each step the thesis asks what happens today, what the step requires, what an agent could do, and where a person should stay involved.
The result places each step somewhere between work that ordinary code should do, work an agent could take on, and work that should remain with the analyst.
It ends with a description of each proposed agent: what it receives, what it produces, what tools it uses, what it must never do, and how one would know it worked.
That description is meant to be the starting point for building the agents in the master's thesis.

The thesis also makes two contributions beyond GGM.
First, it applies to a market equilibrium model a division of labour for which the recent literature offers evidence: language models reason and propose, deterministic code computes, and people decide where decisions are hard to undo.
That evidence comes from engineering simulation and empirical economics; the thesis examines, step by step, how far it carries to a different kind of model.
Second, it examines a case the literature on agent-assisted calibration has not addressed: calibrating a model whose parameters are not uniquely determined by the data and carry economic meaning, so that equally good fits can embody different assumptions about how the market works.
The thesis argues that in such a case a person must remain responsible for accepting the calibration, not only as a safety margin that better models may one day remove, but because the decision is partly an economic judgment.
That argument is developed in Chapters 5 and 7.

The contribution is also practical.
Preparing and calibrating GGM is a documented burden for the researchers who use it, and a clear account of which parts of that work could be automated, and at what risk, is useful to them in its own right.
What the thesis does not contribute is evidence that any particular agent works; that is left to the master's thesis.
