\section{Structure of the thesis}

The thesis has eight chapters.

Chapter 2 reviews the literature on LLM agents that set up, run, calibrate and explain existing domain models, and on how work is divided between agents, code and people, and ends by stating the gap the thesis addresses.

Chapter 3 describes the Global Gas Model: what it represents, how it is structured, what its inputs are, and what the workflow around it looks like.
It ends by identifying where in that workflow the effort and judgment lie.

Chapter 4 explains what LLM agents are and how they differ from a single prompt, what they are good and bad at, which models are available and what they cost, and which kinds of tasks are better left to ordinary code.

Chapter 5 is the core of the thesis.
It walks through the GGM workflow step by step and, drawing on Chapters 3 and 4, asks at each step what an agent could do and where a person must remain involved.
It ends with a summary of where each step lands and a description of each proposed agent.

Chapter 6 sketches the system that the analysis implies: its components, where people sit in the loop, and how its work would be checked.
Chapter 7 discusses the risks, what should not be automated, and the limitations of the analysis.
Chapter 8 summarises what has been established, what remains open, and the plan for the master's thesis.
