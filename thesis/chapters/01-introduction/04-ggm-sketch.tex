\section{The Global Gas Model in brief}

GGM represents the natural gas value chain from production, through pipelines or LNG, to consumption and storage, for more than ninety countries in five-year steps with three seasons per year \citep{egging2019global}.
Suppliers may behave anywhere between perfect competition and Cournot competition, set by a market power parameter for each supplier, node and year.
The equilibrium is computed by solving a single quadratic optimisation problem, in which a market power adjustment term makes the solution correspond to imperfect competition rather than to a welfare maximum \citep[pp.~47, 49]{egging2019global}.

The data for a scenario are held in three spreadsheet workbooks: one describing the world (infrastructure, costs, market power), one with projected production and consumption, and one with calibrated values.
Infrastructure data are collected from databases; projected production and consumption are a scenario storyline, anchored to a published outlook and adjusted by the analysts; and the rest are calibrated: according to the documentation, production costs and capacities, reference prices and market power are adjusted until the model reproduces reference production and consumption, first for the base year and then for future years \citep[p.~26]{egging2019global}.
Because trade links all markets, a change in one country's costs shifts consumption elsewhere, and the analyst ``needs to combine market knowledge with model expertise and trade off choices'' \citep[p.~26]{egging2019global}.
Chapter 3 describes the model and its workflow in full.
