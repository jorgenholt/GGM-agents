Paper 1 of 5 · GGM

Global Gas Model: Model and Data Documentation v3.0

Authors
Ruud Egging and Franziska Holz
Year
2019
Published
DIW Data Documentation 100, DIW Berlin
Link
doi.org/10.18723/diw_ddc:2019-100

Why it matters for us

  • The source of truth for chapter 3: model structure, data sources and calibration, with page numbers we can cite.
  • States that calibration takes an experienced analyst days to weeks (pp. 28–32). The clearest evidence of manual effort worth helping with.
  • Writes down how an expert diagnoses a bad calibration (p. 28). That gives us something to test agents against later.

Paper 2 of 5 · AI and calibration

Agentic Calibration of Grey-Box Simulation Models: An LLM-Driven Alternative

Authors
David Gómez-Guillén, Mireia Díaz, Josep Lluis Arcos, Jesús Cerquides
Year
2026 (July)
Published
Preprint, arXiv:2607.18308
From
Catalan Institute of Oncology and IIIA-CSIC, Spain

Why it matters for us

  • An LLM acts as the optimiser in a calibration loop. It sees past guesses and errors and proposes the next parameter set, with a written reason.
  • It needed 16 model runs, against 110 for Bayesian optimisation and hundreds to thousands for Nelder–Mead.
  • The closest paper to GGM calibration. The difference: its targets are observed data, while GGM's calibrated values include market power, which is judgment.

Paper 3 of 5 · AI and model inputs

AutoSAM: An Agentic Framework for Automating Input File Generation for the SAM Code

Authors
Zaid Abulawi, Zavier Ndum, Eric Cervi, Rui Hu, Yang Liu
Year
2026 (March)
Published
Preprint, arXiv:2603.24736
From
Texas A&M University and Argonne National Laboratory

Why it matters for us

  • Builds input files for a reactor simulation code from PDFs, diagrams and spreadsheets. That is the same kind of work as filling GGM's workbooks from IEA, ENTSOG or GIIGNL reports.
  • Puts every extracted value in a step a person can check, flags missing data and labels assumed values.
  • About 88% of values were extracted correctly from PDF text: useful, but not good enough to skip review.

Paper 4 of 5 · The limits

HydroAgent: Closing the Gap Between Frontier LLMs and Human Experts in Hydrologic Model Calibration

Authors
Zhi Li, Songkun Yan, Jie Cao, Mofan Zhang, Anjiang Wei, Jinwoong Yoo, Yang Hong
Year
2026 (May)
Published
Preprint, arXiv:2605.17792
From
U. Colorado Boulder, U. Oklahoma, Stanford, NASA Goddard

Why it matters for us

  • Tested nine frontier LLMs (Claude, GPT, Gemini) on calibrating the US National Weather Service's flash-flood model.
  • Almost none reached the human expert's result (one model, at one gauge). The authors blame missing domain knowledge, not model size.
  • Keeps our argument honest: today, an agent should help the analyst with calibration, not replace them.

Paper 5 of 5 · Humans in the loop

(Human) Attention Is (Still) All You Need: Human Oversight Makes AI-Assisted Social Science Reliable

Authors
Chen Zhu, Xiaolu Wang, Weilong Zhang
Year
2026 (June)
Published
Preprint, arXiv:2606.12848
From
China Agricultural University and University of Cambridge

Why it matters for us

  • An experiment with 280 AI-assisted economics research runs: with no constraints, a multi-agent setup failed badly in 72% of runs.
  • Three rules cut that to 16%: the LLM only reasons, data work and estimation run as ordinary code, and a person signs off at three points.
  • This is close to the design principle we are working towards for GGM: judgment from the model, arithmetic in code, a person at the key decisions.
